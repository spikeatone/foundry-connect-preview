Their real name, their real options, and a lot less typing.

This release makes the window personal and the app easier to talk to. Connect now shows the young person by their own name, with the real local programs the mentor found around their paths, and hands you back the way to suggest one. In the app, a teen can just talk instead of type, and a note from someone who cares becomes something they can actually explore.

August 23, 2026 · Connect and app build 10 · foundryconnect.me
The window gets personal

Their name, said right New

Connect now speaks in the young person's own name and pronouns, everywhere: the invitation, the recap, the road ahead, the note you write. No more standing-in placeholder. It reads like a window into this person, because it is.

Careful by default: where we don't know someone's pronouns, the copy stays neutral rather than guessing. A real student is never misgendered by the interface.

The road ahead is real now

Open Schools or Funding and you see the actual local programs the mentor tracked down for this student, the union hall, the community college, the registered apprenticeship, with the address and a number to call. Not sample cards standing in for the idea.

Honest when it's thin: if nothing has been verified near them yet, the card says so plainly, instead of filling the space with a placeholder.

Suggest is back on every card

Each school and funding option carries Suggest to them again, and it names the real program: tap it and a note is started for you, "Worth a look: Front Range Community College, Diesel Tech in Fort Collins, CO," ready to send. One tap from spotting a fit to passing it along.

Still their call: the button only appears when the student has turned messages on for you. Their window, their rules.

In the Foundry app · build 10

Just talk. It types for you. New

Every place you'd type, there's now a mic. Tell the mentor how a step went, name a person to add, sign your consent, all by speaking. The words fill in as you talk, and you can still edit before you send. Kids don't type paragraphs; now they don't have to.

Private by design: the speech-to-text happens right on the phone. Your voice never leaves the device.

A suggestion you can actually act on

When someone you trust sends you a note, "Worth a look: the WA Skilled Careers Scholarship," it now carries an Explore with mentor button. Tap it and the mentor reads what the note is really pointing at, a scholarship to apply for, a program to tour, a number to call, and helps you find one concrete next step that fits.

Flexible on purpose: the mentor takes its cue from the note, so a scholarship and a school tour lead to different, sensible moves, not a canned reply.

Testing this build?

Give extra attention to: talking your answers instead of typing (does the mic feel natural in the reflection box?), and Explore with mentor on a note from your people, does the mentor read the suggestion and offer a real next step?

Under the hood

  • A stray glitch in the interview, gone. Once in a while the mentor's reply picked up a bit of stray punctuation from behind the scenes. It's scrubbed now, on both the app and the server, so the conversation always reads clean.
  • A fresh demo starts truly fresh. Resetting for a new walkthrough now clears the inbox too, so last session's notes don't carry into the next one.
