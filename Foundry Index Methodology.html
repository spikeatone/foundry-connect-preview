The Foundry Index

How we rank institutions and what we won't fake

The Foundry Index is our point of view on where a teenager can train for a skilled career and get an honest return. It is not a government or accreditor standard. This page shows exactly how a rank is built, from which data, so you can judge it for yourself.

The five pillars

Every school or program is scored 0–100 — the Foundry Score. Each metric is normalized within a peer group (a welding program is compared to welding programs, not to a nursing school or a university), then combined using published, per-list weights. A rank is just the order of those scores within one published list.

Weights differ by list — an earnings-focused list leans harder on Pillar 1. The exact profile for every published list is shown live below.

The honest-data rules

This is where we diverge from rankings that silently drop or backfill missing data.

  • Missing outcomes become "Emerging," never a fake rank. Federal data suppresses any figure covering fewer than 30 students. Small certificate programs — often exactly our audience's programs — may have no published earnings. Those schools are listed and profiled with an "Emerging / Insufficient Outcome Data" badge in a separate tier, never interpolated above measured programs.
  • We never backfill a program's earnings with whole-school numbers and present it as the program's result. If we only have institution-level data, we say so.
  • Earnings are shown as ranges. A single point estimate implies a precision the data doesn't have.
  • Small samples are disclosed, not penalized. A 22-graduate program isn't pushed down the rank for its size; it carries a confidence note.
  • Data vintage is always shown. "Earnings reflect the 2023–24 cohort" — outcomes are historical, not a promise.

When a pillar's metric is unavailable for a school, the score is computed on the available pillars, re-normalized to 100%, and the profile lists exactly which pillars were and weren't measured. A school is ranked in the main tier only if it has outcome data (Pillar 1 or 2); otherwise it's Emerging.

Emerging schools — not a demotion

An Emerging school is one we couldn't score on outcomes yet — not a school we scored and ranked low. It's the honest label for a data gap, and it matters most for exactly the programs our students care about: small, new, or specialized ones the federal data hasn't caught up to.

  • Why a school lands here. To earn a Foundry Score, a school needs published outcome data — Pillar 1 (earnings) or Pillar 2 (completion/quality). Federal data suppresses any figure covering fewer than 30 students, so a small certificate program often has no published earnings to score. When neither outcome pillar has data, the school is Emerging instead of ranked.
  • It's a gap in public data, not a verdict on the school. A brand-new welding program with 18 graduates can be excellent and still be Emerging — the government simply hasn't published its numbers. Emerging says "we won't pretend to know," never "this school is worse."
  • We never fake a rank to fill the gap. Emerging schools are never interpolated, estimated, or slotted above measured programs. That's the whole point: a made-up rank would be more harmful than an honest "not enough data yet."
  • How it's shown. Emerging schools are still listed and profiled — with an "Emerging / Insufficient Outcome Data" badge, in their own tier below the ranked list. The reader sees the school exists, sees why it isn't ranked, and can still explore it.
  • Schools move out of Emerging as data arrives. When a program graduates enough students for federal outcomes to publish — or an official licensure source covers it — it earns a Foundry Score in the next edition. Emerging is a starting point, not a ceiling.
The short version: "Emerging" means we don't have the outcome data to rank this school honestly — so we say that, plainly, instead of guessing. A ranked school and an Emerging school are answering two different questions: how did its graduates do versus we can't tell you yet.

Every source, with its vintage

College Scorecard — US Dept. of Education
Program-level earnings and debt, keyed by 4-digit CIP code + credential level, plus the school's accreditor code. This is what lets us rank a welding program, not just a whole school. Pooled across multiple years to reduce noise.
Vintage: most recent available cohort; shown per list edition (2027).
IPEDS — US Dept. of Education
Institution characteristics, net price, completion, retention, and program offerings. Every school taking federal aid reports.
Vintage: most recent published reporting year.
Accreditation — federal College Scorecard accreditor code
Across all published lists, the school's accreditor is taken from the federal accreditor_code field (e.g. ACCSC, ABHES, NACCAS, regional). A clean accredited/not signal plus accreditor identity.
Vintage: current federal file.
FAA AMTS — aviation-maintenance licensure
For aviation lists, the licensure pass rate is the FAA's Aviation Maintenance Technician School Minimum Passage Rate report (14 CFR §147.25), covering 8/2023–12/2024: passing students ÷ (passing + failing) on FAA mechanic tests within 60 days of graduation, matched to schools by FSDO region.
Vintage: FAA report through Q4 2024. Officially PRELIMINARY — see the caveat below.
CA Board of Barbering & Cosmetology — cosmetology licensure
For California cosmetology schools, the pass rate is the Board's "Examination Results by School" report, aggregated across license types (cosmetologist, barber, esthetician, manicurist) and weighted by test-takers. Only schools with at least 30 test-takers are included — smaller cohorts are omitted, never guessed.
Vintage: Q4 2024. California only today — see the coverage note below.
DOL RAPIDS — apprenticeship / placement
Program- and sponsor-level apprenticeship completion data (PII removed), used for apprenticeship-route programs and as a benchmark.
Vintage: latest available extract.
On the FAA aviation figures: the FAA passage-rate data is officially preliminary and is not for compliance use until January 2027. We show it because it is the best school-level licensure signal that exists for aviation maintenance, and we label it as preliminary wherever it appears.
Licensure coverage is partial — because the public data is. A licensure exam pass rate exists here only where an official source publishes it school-by-school: nationally for aviation (the FAA), and for cosmetology in California (the state board). Many states publish only statewide aggregates, not per-school results, so a by-school rate simply doesn't exist to cite for them — this coverage is limited by what the sources release, not by effort. Schools without a licensure source are scored on the pillars they do have — Pillar 4 re-normalizes over the available signals rather than penalizing a school for a gap in public data. We add a state or vocation whenever an official body publishes by-school results, and we'd rather leave a metric blank than fabricate one. By design, every licensure rate we show traces to an official government source — the FAA and the California state board — never a third-party estimate or an exam vendor.

Pillar 5 launches at 0% — and is earned in

Pillar 5 (Student Experience) is a flywheel, not a cold input: it only carries signal after Foundry has users, but the Index has to be credible before that. So it is never a launch dependency, and its weight is disclosed here as a versioned roadmap from day one.

  • Ships as a Four-Pillar Index (v1). At launch Pillar 5's weight is 0%; Pillars 1–4 re-normalize to 100%. Student Experience will be introduced in a future edition as verified review volume grows — the roadmap is the transparency, not a gap we hide.
  • Phased in per school, gated by review volume. A school with 0–7 reviews is pulled fully to its peer-group mean — the pillar neither helps nor hurts it. Only with hundreds of verified reviews does Pillar 5 move that school's score. The global weight rises 0% → 5% → 10% across annual editions as aggregate verified-review volume crosses stated thresholds. Thin data never corrupts a ranking.
  • Reviews come from real, verified students — people who actually attended or seriously considered a program, gathered through Foundry's mentor loop and its Connect counselors. Quality over quantity, honestly labeled: 30 verified reviews beat 3,000 anonymous ones. Pillar 5 is small by design (10% cap) and explicitly subjective.

Corrections, and what a score is not

Every Foundry Score is explainable: it opens to a pillar-by-pillar breakdown and the underlying source figures, so a student learns to read outcomes critically — not just trust a number.

Correction process

A school can flag an error. We cite our sources and either fix it or explain why the figure stands. Corrections are part of the process, not an exception to it.

Disclaimer

Scores reflect historical, publicly-reported outcomes and are not a promise of any individual's result. The Foundry Index is Foundry's point of view — not a government, accreditor, or official standard, and it makes no claim of official status.
